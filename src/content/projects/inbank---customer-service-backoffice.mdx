<CaseSection title="Three decisions I would defend" subtitle="what makes the tool faster to use" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 3" data-decision="1" title="Records open beside the customer">
    An agent on a call often needs a contract or an application while the customer's details stay in view. So a record opens in a side panel over the customer page. When the agent closes it, they are back where they were.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="A credit application open in the side panel, with the customer page still behind it." media={img(panel, 'The customer page with a credit application open in a side panel on the right: product, identification and contact details.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 3" data-decision="2" title="Every status sits next to its fix">
    Problems show as status pills at the top of the record: Unsigned, Unidentified, Required. The action that fixes each one sits on the same line, like Identify or Check PEP, so the agent always sees the next step.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="A contract: three statuses next to the title, and Identify next to the customer status." media={img(contract, 'A contract page: Unsigned, Unidentified and Required pills next to the title, and an Identify link next to the customer status.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 3 of 3" data-decision="3" title="One search for every number">
    Customers call with whatever number they have: an ID code, a contract, an account, a username. One search box takes all of them, and each result shows what it matched.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The global search: one query finds the customer by ID code, account numbers and username." media={img(search, 'The global search open: two customers named Sirje Tamm, each with an ID code, account numbers and a username.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="audit" title="Map every screen before drawing one">
    I listed every screen of the old tool and tagged each part: a table, a state, an action, a filter or a pop-up. The same few parts appeared on every screen. These parts became the component list.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Part of the map: each screen with its tables, states, actions and filters." media={img(screenMap, 'A map of the old back-office: Billing, Contracts, Customers, Home and Offer screens, each part tagged as a table, state, action or filter.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="research" title="Ask the people who use it, then check the data" pills={['support agents', 'team leads', 'product managers', 'developers']}>
    I interviewed the people who use the tool or work on it. Mixpanel showed which screens and actions they used most, so I redesigned those first.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="visual direction" title="A moodboard before the screens">
    Before any screen, I made a moodboard that followed Inbank's new visual identity. I used it to set the tone, the hierarchy and the level of consistency early.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The moodboard: data-heavy tools with a calm, clear hierarchy." media={img(moodboard, 'A moodboard of about twenty dashboards, tables, search panels and profile pages.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="delivery" title="Work with the developers during the build">
    I had weekly syncs with the developers and reviewed each build. When a technical limit came up, I changed the design. Several key screens shipped, including flows for the new product lines.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Shipped screens: a contract, a customer with a credit application open, the search, and the parts they share." media={img(shipped, 'Shipped back-office screens on a purple background: a contract on desktop and tablet, a customer page with a side panel, the search, and four cards.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>
