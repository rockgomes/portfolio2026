<CaseSection title="Three decisions I would defend" subtitle="what makes the flow clear in every market" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 3" data-decision="1" title="Mobile sets the structure" pills={['mobile first', 'desktop from the same structure']}>
    Most customers finished the flow on a phone, so mobile was the main constraint. Hierarchy, actions and forms had to stay clear on a small screen first. Desktop layouts grow from the same structure, so there is one flow to build and keep, not two.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The main path on a phone: plan, identification, details, offer, down payment, done." media={img(mobilePath, "Six phone screens of the checkout in a row: Pay in parts, Identify yourself, Hello!, Your personal credit offer, Down payment, and You're all set!")} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 3" data-decision="2" title="The money comes first">
    People who choose financing want to know what it costs. So the screen leads with the most important figures, groups the related ones, and keeps strong colour for the action that moves the customer forward. It is less to read before a decision.

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="audit" title="Map the whole journey first">
    I mapped the full loan application across devices, systems and people: the customer, the merchant's shop, the checkout, the partner portal, the back-office and customer support. The map showed where the flow was split, repeated or hard, most of all on mobile.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" id="map" label="Which map" selected={1} items={[
      { label: 'The map I found', caption: 'How the flow was mapped before I arrived: screens and arrows.', media: img(mapFound, 'A large board of small old checkout screens joined by arrows, in several groups.') },
      { label: 'The map I made', caption: 'My map: every step and decision, by who does it, from the shop to the payout.', media: img(mapMine, 'A swimlane map with six lanes: Personal, Partner eShop, ePOS, Partner portal, Backoffice and Customer support, with steps, decisions and end points.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="research" title="Learn the rules with product and analytics" pills={['country rules', 'data and edge cases', 'new and returning customers']}>
    With product managers and analysts, I worked through the legal rules of each country, the data each step needs and its edge cases, and how new and returning customers differ. The result was one shared picture of the flow and a clear list of what to simplify first.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="flows" title="Country rules inside one structure">
    I organised the flow into the same stages for every market. Where a country needs more, like Estonia's AIS flow, it uses the same screens and patterns, on mobile and desktop. Less duplication, simpler development, and the checkout feels the same in every market.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="tabs" id="flow" label="Flow" items={[
      { label: 'Main flow', caption: 'The main flow in eight stages, from the landing page to the end, with its variants.', media: img(flowMain, 'Phone screens grouped in stages: Landing pages, Identification, Additional info, Decision, Offer, Signing, Downpayment and End.') },
      { label: 'Estonia · AIS flow', caption: 'The Estonian AIS flow, on mobile and desktop, built from the same screens.', media: img(flowAis, 'The Estonian AIS flow on mobile and on desktop: More information needed, Please wait, Add another statement and Thank you screens, with arrows for each path.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="delivery" title="Stay close to the build">
    I worked with product, analytics and engineering to the end: aligning on logic and country rules, changing designs when a technical limit came up, reviewing each build, and fixing edge cases during development.
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>
