<CaseSection title="Two decisions I would defend" subtitle="actions where users already look" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 2" data-decision="1" title="Actions sit next to the data they change" pills={['object actions', 'field actions', 'global actions']}>
    Users look at the data, so the action should be there too. An object gets an Actions menu in its header. A field gets its own edit or open button. The panel gets one main action at the bottom. Users finish simple tasks without leaving the panel.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The animated Figma prototype: object, field and global actions in one panel." media={{ kind: 'video', src: '/media/projects/pipedrive---interactive-panels/app-actions.mp4', poster: '/media/projects/pipedrive---interactive-panels/app-actions.jpg', label: 'Animation of a Trello panel: an object opens, its Actions menu opens, a field gets an edit button on hover, and the main button opens a dropdown and a modal.', width: 1040, height: 950 }} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 2" data-decision="2" title="Key actions first, the rest on demand" pills={['progressive disclosure']}>
    One main button per panel, with more actions in its dropdown. Extra objects stay collapsed, with their status and an Actions menu still in view. The panel stays short, but nothing is hidden.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Collapsed objects keep their status and actions (10). One main action, more in the dropdown (11, 12)." media={img(panelBottom, 'The bottom of the new panel: two collapsed objects with status labels and Actions menus, the Add an order button with a dropdown, and the feedback bar. Numbers 10 to 13 explain the parts.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="research" title="Find out why actions were missed" pills={['usage data', 'expected actions', 'schema limits']}>
    We looked at how users used panels, which actions they expected, how easy the current actions were to find, and the limits of Marketplace apps and schemas. Only around 30 percent of users found the actions. So actions had to move closer to the data.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="patterns" title="Patterns, not screens" pills={['action close to data', 'progressive disclosure', 'consistency across apps', 'scalability']}>
    Partners build many different apps, so I designed patterns that work for any panel, app and data type, not single screens.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The brainstorming board: many panel variants before the patterns were set." media={img(patterns, 'A wide Figma board titled brainstorming exercise: many panel variants side by side, some collapsed, some in a modal.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="prototype and build" title="Show the motion, then stay close to the build">
    Many changes were small, so I made animated prototypes in Figma to show how they work. They aligned design, product and engineering, and found edge cases early. During the build, I refined details to fit the app schema and existing Pipedrive UI.
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="What changed" subtitle="the outcome, and what I took from it" id="result" gap={48}>
  <TwoCol
    left={{ label: 'outcome', items: [

    right={{ label: 'what I learned', items: [
