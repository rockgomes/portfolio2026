<CaseSection title="Three decisions I would defend" subtitle="what made the backoffice easier to use" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 3" data-decision="1" title="Two views on the same orders">
    The cycle only had a board. The board is right when the stage matters most, so people who work on one or two stages kept it. A table is easier to scan, sort by patient and search. With a board, search results scatter across columns. So I designed both on the same data, and people can switch.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The same cycle as a table: sort, filter and search by patient." media={img(table, 'The 28-day cycle as a table: patient name, date of birth, item, received date and a status pill on each row, with search and sorting above.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 3" data-decision="2" title="Three ways to add, one place to start">
    Well works with care homes at three levels: a group that owns homes, a single home, and a unit inside a home. Each needs its own flow. All three start from the same "Add new" choice, so staff don't need to know which form to look for.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption={'The "Add new" choice: care home group, care home or care home unit.'} media={img(addNew, 'A page asking "What would you like to add?" with three cards: Care home group, Care home and Care home unit.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 3 of 3" data-decision="3" title="A unit takes what its home already knows">
    A unit lives inside a care home, so most of its data is already known. The unit flow reuses the home's details, like round times, instead of asking again. Less typing, and fewer chances to enter something that doesn't match.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Adding a unit: the round times come from the care home, and can still be changed for this unit." media={img(unitRounds, 'The Round times step of adding a care home unit: a note says the rounds were inherited from the care home, above six rounds from Morning to Before bed.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="audit" title="Two audits in the first days">
    I went through the live backoffice screen by screen and left notes on everything that looked off. The design lead audited what the devs built on staging. Together, the audits split the problems in two: design issues came to me, build issues went to the devs.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="From my QA page: the cycle dates step, with my notes on what to fix." media={img(qa, 'The cycle dates step of adding a care home, with a calendar and three date fields, next to a yellow note listing what to fix.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="mapping" title="Map the system before drawing">
    Care home groups, homes, units, patients, GPs and orders all connect. With Claude, I turned what I knew into three maps in FigJam: the care home profile, patient states and order stages. Then I checked them with the team. The order stages became the columns of the cycle view.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" label="Map" id="well-maps" items={[
      { label: 'Order stages', caption: 'Order stages, from request to delivery. An exception goes back to FlowRX once it is resolved.', media: img(mapOrders, 'A flow: Requested, Labelling and checking, FlowRX, Ready to dispatch, Dispatched, Delivered. Issue found sends an order from FlowRX to Exceptions, and resolved brings it back. An issue after Delivered leads to Claim failed.') },
      { label: 'Patient states', caption: 'Patient states. Each change also changes what happens to the patient\'s orders.', media: img(mapPatients, 'A flow: a new patient is Active. From Active: move to hospital, move to care home, no longer in home, or mark as deceased. Patient returns goes from Hospitalised back to Active.') },
      { label: 'Care home profile', caption: 'The care home profile and its tabs. The Units tab leads to a unit profile with its own tabs.', media: img(mapProfile, 'A flow: the care homes list opens a care home profile with the tabs Cycle, Items, Patients, Activity, Contacts, GP surgeries and Units. Drill into unit leads from Units to a unit profile with the same tabs except Units.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="delivery" title="Ship as we go">
    There was no big handoff at the end. Each flow went to the devs and shipped, one by one. When a flow needed a better badge, button or table, I fixed it in the design system first, then used it in the flow.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Order details: click any order in the cycle to see its full history of events." media={img(orderDetails, 'An order details window over the cycle: the patient, the package events from ASN received to Delivered, and the item with its quantity and dosage.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>
