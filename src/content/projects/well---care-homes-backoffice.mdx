<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="mapping" title="Map the system before drawing">
    An order goes through many stages before it reaches the care home, and some of them can loop back. With Claude, I turned what I knew into a map of the order stages in FigJam. Then I checked it with the team. The order stages became the columns of the cycle view.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Order stages, from request to delivery. An exception goes back to FlowRX once it is resolved." media={{ kind: 'map', map: orderStages }} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="delivery" title="Ship as we go">
    There was no big handoff at the end. Each part of the cycle went to the devs and shipped, one by one. When it needed a better badge, button or table, I fixed it in the design system first, then used it in the cycle.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Order details: click any order in the cycle to see its full history of events." media={img(orderDetails, 'An order details window over the cycle: the patient, the package events from ASN received to Delivered, and the item with its quantity and dosage.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="A decision I would defend" subtitle="what made the cycle easier to use" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision" data-decision="1" title="Two views on the same orders">
    The cycle only had a board. The board is right when the stage matters most, so people who work on one or two stages kept it. A table is easier to scan, sort by patient and search. With a board, search results scatter across columns. So I designed both on the same data, and people can switch.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The same cycle as a table: sort, filter and search by patient." media={img(table, 'The 28-day cycle as a table: patient name, date of birth, item, received date and a status pill on each row, with search and sorting above.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>
<CaseSection title="What changed" subtitle="the outcome, and what I took from it" id="result" gap={48}>
  <TwoCol
    left={{ label: 'outcome', items: [

    ] }}
    right={{ label: 'what I learned', items: [

      'Talking to the people who use the backoffice showed me where they get stuck. It also showed me what clear means to them: one name for one thing, and nothing left to guess.',
    ] }} />
</CaseSection>
