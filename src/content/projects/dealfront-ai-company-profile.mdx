<CaseSection title="Four decisions I would defend" subtitle="what I chose, and what I gave up" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 4" data-decision="1" title="Questions, not one automatic summary" pills={['internal users', 'real users, same week']}>
    The first design had one Generate button: the AI read the whole log and wrote a summary. Users pushed back. The summary was fine, but it was not what they would have asked. So every block now opens with question cards and a free text field.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" id="design" label="which design" selected={0} items={[
      { label: 'Dropped: Generate', caption: 'The whole flow of the design we dropped.       { label: 'Shipped: question cards', caption: 'The way in is a question someone already wanted to ask.', media: img(shipped, 'The Activity tab with the AI activity summary block: three question cards and a prompt field above the activity data.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 4" data-decision="2" title="Filter the table, do not mark the rows">
    Contacts questions return names, so the answer has to reach the table. I tried an AI icon on the recommended rows, with the reason in a hover. Filtering won. The icon was easy to miss in a long table, and a hover never reaches keyboard or touch users. The toggle is visible, says what it does, and you can undo it.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" id="table" label="how the answer reaches the table" selected={1} items={[
      { label: 'Dropped: icon and hover', caption: 'The one we dropped: the AI marks people in the table, and you hover to find out why.', media: img(flag, 'The contacts table with an AI icon next to two names and a blue tooltip that explains why one person was picked.') },
      { label: 'Shipped: filter toggle', caption: 'Turn it on and only the recommended people show. You can see it, and you can undo it.', media: img(filter, 'The filter toggle turned on, with the table cut down to the two recommended people and their names shown as chips you can remove.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 3 of 4" data-decision="3" title="Show where the answer comes from">
    Round one shipped with a line that says the AI can make mistakes. That hands you the work and no way to do it. In round two, answers come back in numbered sections. On AI Insights, each claim carries a Source link you can open. When the AI finds nothing, the answer says so.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="tabs" id="source" label="where the AI reads" selected={1} items={[
      { label: 'Activity', caption: 'On Activity there are no links, because the data is ours.', media: img(activity, 'An AI activity answer with the question shown above it, key points as bullets, thumbs up and down, and no source links.') },
      { label: 'AI Insights', caption: 'On AI Insights the claims carry links, because the material came from outside. When it finds nothing, it says so.', media: img(insights, 'An AI Insights answer in numbered sections, each claim with a Source link, and one section that says nothing was found.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 4 of 4" data-decision="4" title="Edit a pin, not a question you already asked" pills={['MVP scope']}>
    Claude and ChatGPT let you edit a question and run it again. We left it out: it needs its own states and history, and this was an MVP. Asking again costs a few seconds. Editing a pinned question was built, because you reuse a pin many times.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Built: editing a pinned question. Left out: editing and re-running a question you already asked." media={img(editPin, 'The Edit pinned question window, with the title and prompt filled in, a delete button, and cancel and save buttons.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="two rounds, about a week each" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="round one" title="Research and design side by side" pills={['PMs', 'CS reps', 'clients', 'C-level']}>
    Research, design and testing ran at the same time, not one after another. We had a basic first draft by Tuesday, with no text input yet. Through the week we moved from no questions to people asking their own, and handed it to the dev team on Friday.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="one pattern" title="One block, three places">
    Activity and Contacts run the same block. Only the title and the questions change, because each one reads the data on its tab. AI Insights takes any question about the company and can read outside material too, so it got its own tab.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="It can answer any question about the company, so it got the whole tab." media={img(insightsTab, 'The AI Insights tab with six suggested insight cards in two columns above a prompt field and an Ask AI button.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="round two" title="Join the house pattern, keep the log in view">
    Lists Enrichment and Alerts were building their own AI features, each with its own look. The Company Profile joined one shared pattern. On Activity, the AI block also pushed the log down, so AI summary and Filter now share one space.
