<CaseSection title="Four decisions I would defend" subtitle="what I chose, and what I gave up" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 4" data-decision="1" title="Each AI block starts with questions" pills={['internal users', 'real users, same week']}>
    The first design had one Generate button. The AI read the whole activity log and wrote a summary. Users did not want it. The summary was good enough, but it did not answer the questions they had. So every block now opens with question cards and a text field.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" id="design" label="which design" selected={0} items={[
      { label: 'Dropped: Generate', caption: 'The design we dropped, from start to end. There is one button, and you cannot choose what the AI writes.', media: img(dropped, 'Three states of the dropped design: an AI Summary bar with a Generate button, a loading skeleton, and one finished paragraph of summary text.') },
      { label: 'Shipped: question cards', caption: 'Each card is a question people already wanted to ask.', media: img(shipped, 'The Activity tab with the AI activity summary block: three question cards and a prompt field above the activity data.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 4" data-decision="2" title="A filter shows the recommended contacts">
    Questions on the Contacts tab return names, so the answer has to show in the contacts table. First I tried an AI icon next to each recommended person, with the reason in a hover tooltip. I chose a filter instead. The icon was easy to miss in a long table, and keyboard and touch users cannot open a hover tooltip. The filter toggle is easy to see and has a clear label. You can turn it off again.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" id="table" label="how the answer shows in the table" selected={1} items={[
      { label: 'Dropped: icon and hover', caption: 'The design we dropped: the AI marks people in the table, and you hover to find out why.', media: img(flag, 'The contacts table with an AI icon next to two names and a blue tooltip that explains why one person was picked.') },
      { label: 'Shipped: filter toggle', caption: 'Turn it on and only the recommended people show. Turn it off to see everyone again.', media: img(filter, 'The filter toggle turned on, with the table cut down to the two recommended people and their names shown as chips you can remove.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 3 of 4" data-decision="3" title="Show where the answer comes from">
    Round one shipped with a line that says the AI can make mistakes. That line asks you to check the answer, but gives you no way to check it. In round two, answers come back in numbered sections. On AI Insights, each claim has a Source link you can open. When the AI finds nothing, the answer says that.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="tabs" id="source" label="where the AI reads" selected={1} items={[
      { label: 'Activity', caption: '', media: img(activity, 'An AI activity answer with the question shown above it, key points as bullets, and thumbs up and down.') },
      { label: 'AI Insights', caption: 'On AI Insights each claim has a link, because the information comes from outside sources. When the AI finds nothing, it says so.', media: img(insights, 'An AI Insights answer in numbered sections, each claim with a Source link, and one section that says nothing was found.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 4 of 4" data-decision="4" title="Only pinned questions can be edited" pills={['MVP scope']}>
    Claude and ChatGPT let you edit a question you already asked and run it again. We left this out. It needs its own states and history, and this was an MVP. Asking again takes a few seconds. We did build editing for pinned questions, because people use a pin many times.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="Built: editing a pinned question. Left out: editing and re-running a question you already asked." media={img(editPin, 'The Edit pinned question window, with the title and prompt filled in, a delete button, and cancel and save buttons.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="two rounds, about a week each" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="round one" title="Research and design at the same time" pills={['PMs', 'CS reps', 'clients', 'C-level']}>
    We did research, design and testing at the same time. We had a basic first draft by Tuesday, with no questions and no text field yet. By Friday people could ask their own questions, and we handed the design to the developers.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="one pattern" title="The same AI block on three tabs">
    Activity and Contacts use the same AI block. Only the title and the questions change, because each block reads the data on its own tab. AI Insights answers any question about the company and can also read outside sources, so it got its own tab.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="AI Insights answers any question about the company, so it has the whole tab." media={img(insightsTab, 'The AI Insights tab with six suggested insight cards in two columns above a prompt field and an Ask AI button.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="round two" title="Match the other AI features and keep the log visible">
    Lists Enrichment and Alerts were building their own AI features, each with its own look. We moved the Company Profile to one shared AI style. On Activity, the AI block also pushed the activity log down. So the AI summary and Filter buttons now share one space.
