<CaseSection title="Three decisions I would defend" subtitle="three turning points, in the order they happened" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 3" data-decision="1" title="Views belong to one list" pills={['dropped: a new list every time', 'dropped: views shared across lists', 'chosen: views inside their list']}>
    Users did not want more lists. They wanted more ways to look at one list. So where does a saved filter live? I compared three answers. A new list every time was the problem already. Views shared across all lists break the link: a group like "low engagement" only makes sense next to its own list. So views live inside their own list, and the list stays the source of truth.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="The view selector sits in the list's own toolbar, under the list name." media={img(toolbar, 'The list header: the list name CRM Connected with its size, 29k, and the Default view selector in the same toolbar, next to the filter and column icons.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 3" data-decision="2" title="Filters and columns are one idea">
    I tried the controls in a sidebar, a flyout, a popup, a side panel, and a View options modal with tabs that kept growing wider. The turning point: filters and columns answer one question together, which companies and what you see about them. So they come together at the moment you save.
    <div slot="proof" style={{ maxWidth: '648px' }}>
      <ProofSwitch kind="segmented" id="stage" label="Design stage" selected={1} items={[
        { label: 'Explored', caption: 'A road not taken: a tabbed modal that tried to hold filters and table setup in one place.', media: img(explored, 'A View options modal with two tabs, Filters and Table. The Filters tab has three columns: available filters, the selected filter, and active filters.') },
        { label: 'Shipped', caption: 'Save as new view: the filters and the columns, each with a count, saved as one thing.', media: img(shipped, 'The Save as new view modal: a view name, Sales CRM, then Filters with a count of 2 and Columns with a count of 6.') },
      ]} />
    </div>
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 3 of 3" data-decision="3" title="The state is always honest">
    Views only work if people always know what they are looking at. So the state is never hidden. Change a saved view and a "modified" flag tells you, with save as new or revert. One rule: Default view clears everything, and it is never overwritten by accident.

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="exploration" title="Five places for the controls" pills={['sidebar', 'flyout', 'popup', 'side panel', 'tabbed modal']}>
    Before the final design, I tried the filter controls in five places. The tabbed modal grew wider with every tab, because it tried to fit filters and table setup into one surface. That is what pointed to one idea instead of two.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="filtering" title="Filtering, kept simple">
    The filter icon opens a short Filter window. Each category opens on its own, with a number for its active filters. Clear all and Apply filter close it. Applied filters then show as chips under the toolbar.
