<CaseSection title="Two decisions I would defend" subtitle="where to start, and what to build" id="decisions">
  <CaseBlock label="decision 1 of 2" data-decision="1" title="Start with calling apps" pills={['used very often', 'no good way to sync call data']}>
    The data showed that calling integrations are used very often, and they had no good way to sync call data. So they were the best first case for a deeper integration. A good fit there would prove the idea for other apps.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="decision 2 of 2" data-decision="2" title="Always there, never in the way">
    Users wanted quick, 'permanent' access to the integration, and also a way to get it out of the way. They wanted alerts, and links from the app into Pipedrive, like opening a person's page from a caller. So the app lives in a window that floats, moves freely and minimises.

<CaseSection title="How I got there" subtitle="the process, short" id="process">
  <CaseBlock label="interviews" title="Talk to both sides" pills={['4 or 5 partner calls', '10 to 12 user interviews']}>
    With partners, my team and I talked about the data they could share, pricing models and their experience with other CRMs. With users, I let the conversation lead them to my questions, so I did not have to ask them directly.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="design rounds" title="Get the whole design team on board">
    The idea changed the core product, so it needed the whole design team. I shared drafts with engineers, the PM and other designers all the time. It took many rounds.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="testing" title="Four prototypes, two per interview" pills={['4 prototypes', '2 per interview', 'order changed each time']}>
    Each prototype tested a different idea, for example a different entry point. In each interview, users did the same task in two prototypes and compared them. The order changed in every interview.
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>
