<CaseSection title="Four things it does differently" subtitle="try each one in the demo" id="differently">
  <CaseBlock label="1 of 4" title="206 selected, 173 still match">
    You select all 206 matching rows, then tighten the filter. Only 173 still match. The app cannot know which one you meant, so it asks: keep all 206, trim to 173, or start over.
    <ProofSwitch slot="proof" kind="segmented" id="selection" label="Show the selection before or after the filter changed" selected={1} items={[
      { label: 'Selected', caption: 'All 206 matching rows selected, in the bar at the bottom.', media: img(selected, 'The Fieldset table with every row ticked and a bar at the bottom: 206 rows selected.') },
      { label: 'Filter changed', caption: 'Headcount changed from 200 to 900. The app shows both numbers and asks.', media: img(filterChanged, 'Headcount is now more than 900. A bar over the table says 173 of the 206 selected rows still match, with Keep all 206 and Trim to 173 matching.') },
    ]} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="2 of 4" title="Edits survive a switch">
    Edit a saved view, switch to another, then come back. Your edits are still there, marked unsaved, with the saved version one click away.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="3 of 4" title="A deleted field does not break a view">
    A saved view can point at a field that no longer exists. The view still works: it flags the dead condition, skips it, and lets you point it somewhere else.
    <Proof slot="proof" caption="EMEA legacy: one condition is ignored because its field was deleted." media={img(emea, 'The EMEA legacy view: its first condition uses region_emea (deleted) and is marked as ignored. The table still shows 3100 rows.')} />
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="4 of 4" title="A way back that cannot break">
    “All companies” cannot be renamed, deleted or saved over. It is the way back. I added it after I saved over it by accident.
  </CaseBlock>
  <CaseBlock label="how it is built" title="The logic has no React near it" pills={['React', 'TypeScript', 'Vitest', 'no backend']}>
    React and TypeScript, no backend. The 50,000 rows are made in your browser, the same every time, and only about 30 exist at once. Filtering, sorting, selection and saved views are plain functions, so the tricky parts have tests.
  </CaseBlock>
</CaseSection>
